# The calibration conversation — what to ask for, and what to ask

**Written 18 September 2026.** For reading aloud on a call, and for running the
session afterwards.

> **The one-line ask:** *"Half an hour with whoever knows your clients best, in
> front of a laptop, with your contact list and two or three real properties.
> At the end of it the system knows what a good match looks like for you
> specifically, instead of guessing."*

---

# 1. What to say it is

Not "configuration", not "onboarding", not "setting some parameters". Those all
sound like our work that they have to sit through.

**It is the half hour where their judgement goes into the system.** Everything
else about the product they could, in principle, get somewhere else. This is the
part that is theirs — how *this* agency decides a property is worth showing to a
particular buyer — and nobody else has it.

The honest reason it has to happen at all, if they ask: **we refuse to guess.**
There is no industry-standard number for "how far over budget is still worth a
phone call". It depends on the market and on how the agency works, and anything
we picked would be a guess wearing their name. So the system refuses to match
anybody until they have answered, and it says so plainly on screen rather than
quietly inventing a default.

---

# 2. What they need to have with them

| | |
|---|---|
| **A person** | Whoever actually knows the clients — the agent, not the office manager. If the answers are "I'd have to ask", the wrong person is in the room |
| **Their contact list** | Whatever form it is in. A spreadsheet, an export from whatever they use, even the contacts on a phone. **Do not ask them to tidy it first** — an agency that has to reformat a file before onboarding does not onboard |
| **Two or three real properties** | Real ones, currently on their books, with the real asking price. Not examples |
| **Half an hour** | For the questions in §4. The list conversation in §5 is separate and longer — see §6 |

**On the contact list, if they ask how many:** anything from fifty upward is
useful; a few hundred is normal; it does not need to be complete. What matters
is that it is *real*, because the whole point is that the answers get checked
against their actual clients rather than against invented ones.

**If they have no listings on their books this week**, the session still works —
the questions in §4 are about how they think, not about a specific property. But
it is much better with real ones, because the answers get concrete.

---

# 3. What they get out of it, said before the questions

Worth saying at the start, because eight questions with no stated purpose feels
like paperwork:

- **From that moment the system can tell them who a new property suits**, and
  why, in their contacts' own words
- **They see the silence**: who told the agency what they were looking for and
  was then never spoken to again. That number usually lands
- They can change any answer later, in one screen, in seconds

---

# 4. The eight questions, in their words

These are the questions on screen, in the order they appear. They answer in
euros and in plain numbers; the system works out the rest. **Nothing is
pre-filled** — no suggestions, no examples in the boxes — because a pre-filled
answer collects a click rather than a decision.

### Money

**1.** *"Think of someone who tells you their limit is a certain amount. What
amount shall we use?"*
— any round figure typical for them. €2,000,000 in Cascais, €400,000 elsewhere.

**2.** *"What is the most you would still show that person?"*
— the answer is usually a little above the stated limit, and they will have a
number for it straight away.

**3.** *"And if that same person had said 'we could stretch for the right
place' — how high then?"*
— this is the one that produces the match a form-field search can never make.

### Typology

**4.** *"Someone asks you for a three-bedroom. Would you show them a
two-bedroom?"*
— yes or no. Not "it depends"; push gently for the usual case.

### When it is worth showing

**5.** *"Think of the things a buyer asks you for — the area, the number of
rooms, a garden, parking. How many does a typical buyer list?"*
— usually three or four.

**6.** *"Of those, how many does a property have to have before you would ring
them and say this one is really for you?"*

**7.** *"And how many before it is still worth sending, without making a
fuss about it?"*
— this one must be a lower number than the last. The screen will say so if it
is not.

### Areas

**8.** *"Which areas do your buyers treat as interchangeable? Someone who asks
for Cascais — where else would they happily look?"*
— written one line per area: `Cascais: Estoril, Parede`. It works in both
directions, so they only say it once. **"None" is a real answer** and some
agencies will give it.

---

# 5. The other conversation, which is longer and is not this one

**Do not promise both in half an hour.** They are different sessions and the
second one is the hard one.

Before a single message can be sent to anybody, **the agency has to say where
each group of contacts came from** — who transacted with them, who enquired and
never bought, who gave written permission and where that record is, and who they
simply no longer know the origin of. The system proposes; the agency declares;
the declaration is recorded with their name and the date, because that record is
what a regulator would ask for.

That is an afternoon, not half an hour, and it is a person going through their
own list group by group. The calibration questions in §4 do not depend on it —
matching works without it — but **nothing is ever sent to anybody until it has
happened.**

If they ask why we cannot just assume: because a contact whose origin nobody can
account for cannot lawfully be messaged, and guessing on their behalf puts the
consequence on their business rather than ours.

---

# 6. How to sequence it on the call

1. **Book the half hour** for §4. That is the concrete ask, it is short, and it
   produces something they can see working the same day.
2. **Mention §5 exists** and that it is a separate, longer sitting before
   anything goes out. Do not bundle it into the same booking — a half-hour ask
   that turns out to be an afternoon is the last easy thing you get to ask for.
3. **Ask them to bring the list and the properties** to the first session
   anyway. Even without §5 it makes the matching real rather than theoretical,
   and the silence number in §3 needs it.

---

# 7. What happens on our side afterwards

For the record, so the follow-up is not improvised either:

- The eight answers are stored against that client with the date and the name of
  whoever gave them, so in six months they can be shown their own answer rather
  than a percentage
- Matching starts working immediately for any contact the system holds stated
  criteria for
- Contacts it cannot rank go to the triage screen instead, where the agent
  chooses by hand and the system keeps what they decided
- **Three things can only be judged in that room**, and they are the reason it
  is a meeting rather than a form: whether the matches are actually right,
  whether the agent would act on the notification or delete it, and whether it
  reads well on a phone. Write down what they say about all three.
